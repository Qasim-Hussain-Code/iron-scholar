# The Tutorial Library
### Free, curated, beginner-first, built to last a lifetime of lifting

This is the companion to your 7-day plan and The Iron Scholar app. It answers one
question completely: for every machine and movement you will ever touch, who
teaches it best for free, and what do you graduate to next.

---

## 0. Why this is organized by pattern, not machine number

C5 and B2 exist only inside one particular gym. But C5 is a horizontal push
and B2 is a vertical pull, and those patterns exist in every gym on earth, forever.
Learn the roughly ten human movement patterns once and you can walk into any gym,
in any country, at any age, read the floor in five minutes, and train well. That
is the actual life-long skill. The machine codes below are still mapped for you,
but the patterns are the curriculum.

---

## 1. How to learn a lift so the tutorial actually sticks

**The three-touch method, for every new exercise:**
1. **Night before:** watch the full tutorial once, slowly (5 to 10 min).
2. **At the machine:** 60-second skim of the same video to refresh the two or
   three key cues, then do your practice set.
3. **After your second working set:** film one set from the side (prop your phone
   against a water bottle). Compare against the video after the session, not during.

**One cue per session.** Trying to fix five things at once fixes nothing. Pick the
single biggest issue from your film, make that the only thought next session.

**Re-film monthly.** Form drifts as weights climb; everyone's does. A monthly
side-view video of your main lifts is the cheapest coaching that exists.

**When to spend money once:** around weeks 9 to 12, when you move to the barbell
bench press and barbell squat, one or two single sessions with a trainer at your
gym purely for those two lifts is the best money in fitness. Everything else in
this library is free.

## 2. The teachers: who to trust for what

Six channels cover an entire lifting lifetime. Links open a YouTube search that
lands on the channel; these never break, unlike direct video links which rot as
creators re-upload and improve their content.

| Teacher | Find them | Specialty | Use them for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scott Herman Fitness** | [search](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=scott+herman+fitness) | The original encyclopedia: a huge library of clear, no-fluff machine and dumbbell tutorials | Your **first watch** for nearly every machine and dumbbell movement in the plan |
| **Jeff Nippard** | [search](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=jeff+nippard) | Science-based technique deep dives (biochemistry degree, competitive powerlifter) | Your **second watch**: the "technique" videos when a lift matters or a plateau appears |
| **Renaissance Periodization (Dr. Mike Israetel)** | [search](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=renaissance+periodization) | Exercise execution and effort: how deep, how slow, how close to failure | Learning what a **truly effective rep** feels like; his exercise tutorials are gold for hypertrophy |
| **Squat University (Dr. Aaron Horschig, DPT)** | [search](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=squat+university) | Physical therapist: pain-proofing, mobility, fixing aches | The **when something hurts** channel: knee, hip, shoulder, low back |
| **Alan Thrall (Untamed Strength)** | [search](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=alan+thrall+untamed+strength) | Plain-spoken classic barbell teaching | Your weeks 9 to 12 graduation: **squat, bench, deadlift, overhead press, row** |
| **Milo Wolf** | [search](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=milo+wolf+lifting) | Newer research-driven channel (PhD in sports science) | Staying current in year 2 and beyond |

Rule of thumb for the rest of your life: Herman to learn it, Nippard to refine it,
RP to make it grow, Squat University to keep it painless, Thrall for the barbell.

## 5. The 30-second in-gym references

When you are standing at a machine and have half a minute, not ten:

1. **StrengthLog exercise directory**, strengthlog.com/exercise-directory. Free
   directory of 300 to 450+ exercises sorted by muscle group, each with a video
   demonstration, written steps, and a muscles-worked map. The single best
   quick-reference on the internet, and their free app shows the same demos
   while you log.
2. **MuscleWiki**, musclewiki.com. Tap the muscle on a body map, get the demo.
3. **ExRx directory**, exrx.net/Lists/Directory. The old academic standard when
   you want anatomy detail.
4. **Hevy's built-in demos**, if you log there: every exercise has an animation
   one tap away.

## 6. The life-long ladder

**Year 1, where you are.** Run the 7-day plan. By month 3, own every machine
pattern. Months 3 to 12, graduate one barbell lift at a time with Thrall's
tutorials, in this order: bench press, back squat, Romanian deadlift, overhead
press, barbell row, conventional deadlift. Milestones worth chasing: first
strict pull-up, bodyweight on the bench for 5, 1.25x bodyweight squat for 5,
65 kg on the scale.

**Year 2, the graduate school.** Your newbie linear gains slow; you move to
structured programming. The free canon lives at **thefitness.wiki** (the
r/Fitness wiki): start from its Recommended Routines page, where the classic
path is the Basic Beginner Routine into GZCLP or 5/3/1 for Beginners, with the
Reddit PPL as the muscle-focused alternative. The **Boostcamp** app carries
many of these same proven programs, free, with built-in logging. Or return to
me with a year of logs and we write your year-2 program together.

**Year 3 and beyond, the professorship.** Learn to program for yourself:
RP's YouTube concepts (volume landmarks, effort, periodization),
**Stronger by Science** long-form articles, and the **Iron Culture podcast**
(Eric Helms) for the scholarly deep end, ideal treadmill listening. At this
point you will read training studies the way you read methods sections, and
your bioinformatics brain will feel right at home.

**Any year, when something aches:** Squat University first, a sports physician
if it persists. Never train through sharp pain, at 25 or at 65.

## 8. The eight rules that never change

Good form on any exercise, any machine, any decade, reduces to these:

1. Control the lowering phase, two to three seconds, always.
2. Full range of motion that is comfortable and pain-free; deep stretch, full squeeze.
3. A stable base: feet planted, shoulder blades set, spine neutral or deliberately braced.
4. Exhale on effort, brace before heavy attempts.
5. The joint moves the way it was built to; pain that is sharp, sudden, or inside
   a joint is a stop signal, not a challenge.
6. Add load only when every rep of every set is clean.
7. When in doubt, film it from the side.
8. The best exercise is the one you can feel in the target muscle and repeat for
   ten years without injury.

Print this page, or keep it in the app. It will still be true when you are 60.
